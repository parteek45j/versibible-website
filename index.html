The understanding part

Most people stop reading because they never understood it, not because they didn't care. VersiBible fixes the understanding part.

It is not a reading plan that assumes you already know the shape of the thing. It starts where you are, tells you what a passage meant to the people who first heard it, and leaves you with one clear takeaway before you put the phone down.

How a day works

One passage, paced in three beats.

About sixty seconds, start to finish. You tap through it at your own speed — nothing auto-advances, nothing runs out.

1

The verse, alone

Full-bleed, nothing else on the screen. Read it once before anyone explains it to you.

2

The condensed version

The passage in short, with the one thing it is actually saying.

3

The whole card, to sit with

Context, the cross-references it leans on, and a question worth carrying into the day.

Every day, free

The part you use daily costs nothing.

Reading, the daily card, your plan, the library, and search are the free tier — not a trial of it.

Today

One thing to do today.

Progress is shown, never scored. There is no streak counter, no percentage, nothing to break — just today's card and where you are on your path.

Miss a week and the timeline sits exactly where you left it. The app has no opinion about your gaps.

Learning paths

A guided route through a book.

The Gospel of John is twenty-one chapters, about sixty seconds each, sequenced around the goal you picked when you started. Finished chapters stay marked; the next one waits with a single Start.

The Library

All sixty-six books, in order.

Each section carries a plain-language descriptor — the foundation, Genesis to Deuteronomy — so the shelf explains itself. Books you have worked through show their progress inline, which quietly doubles as your literacy map.

The Reader

Reading, with nothing in the way.

A clean page, verse numbers set small, and a page-by-page turn instead of an endless scroll. Ships with the Berean Standard Bible, plus the World English Bible and the King James — all public domain.

Chapter text and study sources come from the public-domain Free Use Bible API.

Search

Passages first. Never an answer box.

Type a word and you get the verses that contain it, with the word marked in place — then topics underneath. The explanation is always one tap further in, so you read the text before you read anyone's opinion of it.

VersiBible Plus

When you want to go further.

Everything above stays free. Plus adds the tools for the days you want more than sixty seconds.

AI study mentor

A conversation that stays tied to the text.

Ask what a passage means and you get an answer built on it, with the references it is drawing from named in line. Where the text does not settle a question, it says so rather than inventing an answer.

Deep Study

History and commentary, side by side.

Who the passage was written to, what surrounds it, and what the classical commentators made of it. Where readings genuinely differ, the disagreement is stated plainly instead of resolved for you.

Word-by-word Greek and Hebrew — lemma, Strong's number, transliteration and morphology — is on the way, drawn from the open STEPBible dataset. Until every word resolves to a real entry, the app shows nothing there rather than inventing it.

Literacy map

Sixty-six marks. One per book.

Amber deepens as a book becomes familiar. The scale is where you started — the map opens with the answer you gave on day one — so it measures growth in understanding rather than days in a row.

Milestones

A quiet sentence when you finish a book.

No confetti, no badge, no share sheet demanding a victory lap. One honest line, the chapters it took, the days it took, and a way back to your map.

What this app refuses to do

No streaks. No scores. No guilt.

Nothing to break

There is no streak, because a streak turns a missed Tuesday into a reason to stop altogether. Come back after a month and the app simply picks up where you were.

Nothing to compete in

No leaderboard, no points, no ranking against strangers. The only comparison the app makes is with the person you were when you started.

Nothing invented

Explanations are tied to the passage and its sources. Where scholars disagree, you are told they disagree. Where the data isn't ready, the screen stays empty.
